# Copyright, Licensing and Platform Policy Risks for Anime Creators (YouTube/TikTok), as of 2026-11-13

Scope note: research date is 2026-11-13. Not legal advice. "Official policy" = text from platform/government/rights-holder pages. "Community/industry commentary" = blogs, creator videos, forums; treat as folklore unless backed by official text. About 18 tool calls were used; several sub-questions (Crunchyroll/Muse/Medialink/Bilibili/Netflix programs, CODA specifics, UU ITE, TikTok Indonesia availability) could not be verified and are listed under Gaps.

## 1. How aggressive are anime rights holders on YouTube/TikTok, and what gets claimed vs left alone?

### Takeaway
Raw or lightly edited anime footage is routinely claimed via Content ID (revenue diverted), and some Japanese rights holders (notably Toei) have issued formal strikes against reviewers and recap/reaction channels. Enforcement is uneven and studio-specific; there is no length of clip that is "safe". Anti-piracy bodies like CODA mostly target pirate sites, but takedown compliance on YouTube/TikTok is very high.

### Cited Findings
- Content ID mechanics (industry commentary, vidIQ, Apr 15 2026): a match lets the rights holder block, track, or monetize; for anime the most common outcome is claiming ad revenue; claims are not strikes, but a lost dispute can escalate to a strike. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/can-you-monetize-anime-clips-recaps-gameplay-youtube/)
- vidIQ states raw anime clips "get Content ID claimed within hours", that most major studios register content in Content ID, that "Toei has actively struck recap channels" while "other studios may claim revenue but leave videos up". This is a marketing-blog assertion, not primary-source data. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/can-you-monetize-anime-clips-recaps-gameplay-youtube/)
- vidIQ lists "what gets claimed or struck": extended scenes with original audio, complete fights/transformations, narration that merely describes the screen, covering a full episode/arc so viewers need not watch. "What successful recap channels do": minimal, brief, muted footage; own voice narration; analysis/criticism; original graphics. (Commentary, not official policy.) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/can-you-monetize-anime-clips-recaps-gameplay-youtube/)
- YouTube official: 1 copyright strike = content removed; 3 strikes = account and associated channels subject to termination; a removed live stream earns a strike plus 7-day live-stream restriction. — [YouTube Help: Understand copyright strikes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)
- Toei Animation official US FAQ: does not support individuals uploading long-form/full video content from its IP "for any purposes regardless of the number of followers"; tells users to check YouTube policies before including any footage; says unauthorized use "would result in the blocking or deletion of your video"; does not grant individuals permission to use characters for creative use or sales. — [Toei Animation USA FAQ](https://www.toei-animation-usa.com/faq.html)
- Notable case: in Dec 2021 Toei hit anime essayist Totally Not Mark (about 700k subs) with 150+ claims/strikes on videos using footage from Dragon Ball episodes/films. In Jan 2022 YouTube reinstated most videos outside Japan after Toei, per the creator, did not respond with a justification to the dispute process and instead manually claimed/blocked each video; creator said YouTube judged the videos could reasonably qualify for fair use in most of the world outside Japan. Single-source (Dexerto relaying the creator's own account). — [Dexerto, Jan 26 2022](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/youtuber-celebrates-landmark-victory-over-toei-animation-after-150-copyright-strikes-1749317/)
- Further Toei strike coverage exists (Uncle Roger, Sept 2023; Digital Music News on a resulting YouTube rule change, Jan 2022); I saw only search snippets, not full articles. — [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interest/2023-09-01/toei-animation-copyright-strikes-youtuber-uncle-roger/.201857); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2022/01/30/anime-youtuber-battle-against-copyright/)
- Historic litigation: Crunchyroll and TV Tokyo sued 13 YouTube anime uploaders (2011). Old, but shows suits are possible, not only claims. — [Crunchyroll news 2011](https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/latest/2011/9/21/crunchyroll-tv-tokyo-sue-13-youtube-anime-uploaders)
- Recent claims of fresh strike waves against anime reviewers/reaction channels (e.g. "90+ strikes", Toei reportedly claiming its own upload by mistake) appear only as Facebook/Instagram/Reddit snippets with no verifiable primary source or date; treat as unverified folklore. — [Facebook snippet](https://www.facebook.com/mastarmedia/videos/these-anime-channels-are-getting-obliterated/1018979957853700/); [Instagram snippet](https://www.instagram.com/p/DYPdxhJNhKq/?hl=en)
- CODA (Content Overseas Distribution Association): Japanese trade body of content holders cooperating on anti-piracy measures to promote overseas expansion; established August 2002. — [CODA](https://coda-cj.jp/en/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_Overseas_Distribution_Association)
- CBR (June 11 2025) reports on rising takedown activity; its snippet gives deletion/compliance rates of 97.84% (YouTube), 99.87% (TikTok), 99.75% (Google Video), 100% (Vimeo) for anime takedown requests. Full article text not retrievable; figure should be re-verified against the underlying transparency/CODA report before quoting. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/anime-streaming-delete-anti-piracy-compliance/)
- Reported (snippet only, unverified): Japan shut down 84 international anime piracy sites as of March 2025; CODA announced an MoU with "IP House", a US copyright takedown enforcement organization. — [Facebook snippet](https://www.facebook.com/hypekageofficial/posts/japanese-anime-manga-and-games-are-getting-a-stronger-anti-piracy-push-in-the-us/1128329792870098/); [X post snippet](https://x.com/animeupdates/status/2095106905957966300?lang=en)

### Inferences
- CODA's public activity is aimed at pirate sites and bulk takedowns, not at individual commentary channels; the practical threat to reviewers comes from individual studios (Toei is the best-documented) and automated Content ID.
- Highest-risk formats: raw clips, long scenes with source audio, compilations, full-episode "recaps" with weak commentary, dubs/subtitled re-uploads. Lower-risk: essays/reviews with brief, muted, clearly commented footage, or mostly original visuals. Reaction videos are in between, regularly claimed regardless of merit (per vidIQ).
- Because claims are automated and Japan has no flexible fair use, "legal argument" and "monetizable" are separate questions.

### Gaps
- No primary-source enforcement statistics per studio (Toei, Toho, Kadokawa, Aniplex, Netflix); only anecdotes.
- Could not verify specific 2025-2026 channel-termination cases with named dates and primary sources.
- CODA's own press releases on YouTube/TikTok actions were not fetched.

## 2. YouTube Partner Program: thresholds, inauthentic/mass-produced and reused content, AI rules

### Takeaway
Current YPP entry is 1,000 subs plus either 4,000 public watch hours/12 months or 10M Shorts views/90 days. The July 15 2025 rename to "inauthentic content" targeted mass-produced/repetitive/template content and, per YouTube, did not change the reused-content policy (commentary, clips, compilations, reactions). Notably, YouTube has announced higher new-entrant thresholds starting Feb 1 2027. A recap channel built on templates plus minimal commentary is exposed on both policies.

### Cited Findings
- Current YPP eligibility (page displayed 2026-11-13): follow channel monetization policies; live in an eligible country; no active Community Guidelines strikes; 2-Step Verification; advanced features access; one active AdSense. Thresholds: 1,000 subs + 4,000 qualified public watch hours in last 12 months, OR 1,000 subs + 10M qualified Shorts views in last 90 days. Shorts-feed watch hours do not count toward the 4,000. Review takes about 1 month; if rejected, re-apply after 30 days (90 days for repeat rejections); appeal within 21 days. Monetization may be turned off for channels inactive 6+ months. — [YouTube Help: YPP overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- Lower tier (fan funding/earn-early features): at 500 subs plus either 3,000 watch hours/year or 3M Shorts views/90 days, creators can access fan funding, Creator Partnerships and YouTube Shopping (stated as "no changes" in the 2027 update). — [YouTube Help: Changes to YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- NEW, effective Feb 1 2027 (announced; accept updated terms by Jan 31 2027): entry for new creators rises to 1,000 subs plus 8,000 qualified watch hours/365 days OR 20M qualified Shorts views/90 days; existing YPP members' status unaffected. Shorts Creator Pool earnings will require 10M qualified Shorts views over last 90 days (missing it does not remove you from YPP). Activity rule: active if 1,000 watch hours/365 days, or 1M Shorts views/90 days, or 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts per 90 days. Premium Lite expands globally. — [YouTube Help: Changes to YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- July 15 2025 update: "repetitious content" renamed "inauthentic content" to clarify it covers content that is "repetitive or mass-produced"; YouTube says this type "has always been ineligible"; "There is no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos." — [YouTube Help: Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Official examples of what cannot monetize (non-exhaustive): similar/repetitive content with low educational value, commentary or minimal variation; same situation/outcome template across videos; image slideshows, templated storylines or scrolling text with minimal narrative/commentary/educational value; AI-generated content using generic/unoriginal templates giving an impression of mass production without the creator's original insight. Channels where content feels "interchangeable from video to video" cannot monetize. — [YouTube Help: Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube's creator-response post (7/10/2025): policy applies "regardless of how the content was made"; reused content "can continue to monetize if you've added significant original commentary, modifications, or educational or entertainment value"; AI tools are welcome and AI-using channels remain eligible; creators must disclose realistic altered/synthetic content. — [YouTube Community: Response to creator questions about YPP policies (July 2025)](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251/response-to-creator-questions-about-ypp-policies-july-2025?hl=en)
- Reused-content wording: allowed if "viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video"; example allowed: "using clips for a critical review". — [YouTube Help: Channel monetization policies (search excerpt)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [YouTube Help FAQ: Reused content & YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/community-guide/271248162/%F0%9F%94%8E-faq-reused-content-youtube%E2%80%99s-partner-program?hl=en)
- Monetization policy compliance is required for YPP; losing monetization happens "regardless of watch hours and subscriber count" when policies are violated. — [YouTube Help: YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- Content ID claim vs YPP: a claim redirects that video's revenue to the claimant while YPP status stays intact (commentary). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/can-you-monetize-anime-clips-recaps-gameplay-youtube/)
- Dispute process per vidIQ: dispute; claimant has 30 days to respond; appeal; last resort is counter-notification requiring real name/address and exposing you to suit. Official page on strikes was fetched only in part. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/can-you-monetize-anime-clips-recaps-gameplay-youtube/)

### Inferences
- For anime recap channels, the dominant risk is two-layered: (1) copyright (claims/strikes), (2) YPP "reused"/"inauthentic" review of the channel as a whole. Repeating a fixed recap template with minimal commentary over footage, particularly with AI voiceover and auto-generated scripts, matches YouTube's own negative examples.
- Original narration, analysis, original visuals and varied structure align with YouTube's stated "meaningful difference" test, but satisfy only the monetization policy, not copyright.
- Anyone planning to apply for YPP should do so before Feb 1 2027 if close to current thresholds, since new-entrant thresholds roughly double.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve YouTube's dedicated AI/altered-content disclosure page or the full "reused content" page text (only excerpts); no verified detail on how often anime recap channels were actually demonetized after July 2025.
- YouTube's Content ID dispute timelines were sourced from vidIQ, not the official help page.

## 3. Official creator programs / clip-sharing guidelines / affiliate programs

### Takeaway
Only a few anime-specific, officially documented creator licences were found. The clearest is Kadokawa's per-title derivative-creation guideline run through a YouTube MCN "Creator Support Program" (registration, credit line, 10-minute cap, revenue share). Toei explicitly offers no individual permission. For Crunchyroll, Muse Indonesia, Medialink, Bilibili, Netflix, Aniplex, Toho: no verified creator licence found.

### Cited Findings
- Kadokawa guideline for "Jashinchan Dropkick X" (YouTube community post, date not shown): permits clipping videos with comments, commentary/introductory videos, and MAD/AMV on YouTube and Niconico without prior consent; register contact info per YouTube channel; max 10 minutes of licensed content per derivative work; required credit legend and Copyright ID; to monetize you must be in YPP and sign up to Kadokawa's "Creator Support Program" (Kadokawa as designated MCN) and pay a copyright fee and service fee out of revenue; a Content ID "copyright claim" from the "KADOKAWA Creator Support Program" is expected and should NOT be disputed; not for paid-membership-only content; prohibits as-is non-creative use, promoting third-party products, misleading "official" impressions; may demand suspension without reason; governed by Japanese law with Tokyo arbitration for non-residents; applies only to the 12 episodes of season X, not earlier seasons. — [Kadokawa guideline on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/post/UgkxrV_roLsDR3b1TZX0Uk__-BU89-kut2Vx)
- CBR (Aug 1 2025) reports "a new content agreement between YouTube and Kadokawa guarantees permission to create secondary content for several popular anime shows". I only retrieved the metadata blurb, not the body, so title list and conditions are unverified. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/anime-kadokawa-youtube-change/)
- Toei Animation (official FAQ): no permission for individuals to use characters/footage; no support for uploading long-form/full content; will "take strict action". — [Toei Animation USA FAQ](https://www.toei-animation-usa.com/faq.html)
- Crunchyroll store affiliate program (via Sovrn merchant page): affiliate rules say "Do not reproduce anime clips, artwork, logos, or product photography without appropriate permission." That is a merchandise affiliate program, not a clip licence. — [Sovrn: Crunchyroll Affiliate Program](https://merchants.sovrn.com/merchants/217994/crunchyroll-affiliate-program)
- Crunchyroll Ambassador program (2012, free 90-day membership for promotion) is historical; current status unknown. — [Crunchyroll 2012](https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/latest/2012/5/30/crunchyroll-launches-ambassador-program)
- Muse Indonesia is part of Muse Communication Co., Ltd. (founded 1992), a licensed anime distributor that streams anime free on YouTube channels (Muse Asia/Indonesia); that is a licensed distributor channel, not a creator-sharing programme. Titles are periodically removed when licences end (Reddit/YouTube snippets). — [Muse Indonesia channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCxxnxya_32jcKj4yN1_kD7A); [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/anime/comments/126hnp4/did_something_happen_to_the_muse_asia_channel_on/?tl=id)
- Analogous non-anime precedent (games): Bandai Namco's video policy allows non-commercial gameplay videos by individuals, tolerates YPP/Super Chat revenue but reserves right to demand it be switched off; Capcom fan content guidelines (Nov 4 2025) bar use of materials "as is or without creative input". Not anime-footage licences, but show the template Japanese publishers use. — [Bandai Namco Video Policy](https://www.bandainamcoent.co.jp/english/videopolicy/); [Capcom Fan Content Guidelines](https://www.capcom-games.com/en/fan-content-guidelines/)
- Anime convention creator badges (Anime NYC, Anime Frontier) offer affiliate links/perks but are event programs, not content licences. — [Anime NYC](https://animenyc.com/applications/content-creator/)

### Inferences
- Where a licence exists it is per-title, registration-based, capped (e.g. 10 minutes) and revenue-shared; creators should look for "guideline"/"ガイドライン"/"二次創作"/"Creator Support Program" notices on each series' official site rather than assume a studio-wide policy.
- A Content ID claim from a licensor's MCN is by design; disputing it can jeopardize the licence (Kadokawa explicitly says not to).

### Gaps
- No verified official page found for: Crunchyroll creator/clip programme, Muse Indonesia/Medialink creator terms, Bilibili anime creator licensing, Netflix anime clip/creator policy, Aniplex, Toho, or TOHO animation fan guidelines. Absence of search hits is not proof none exist.
- Kadokawa-YouTube 2025 agreement details (which titles, fee terms) unverified.

## 4. TikTok monetization routes and copyright constraints

### Takeaway
TikTok's Creator Rewards Program (CRP) pays for original videos of 1+ minute and requires 10k followers and 100k views/30 days, in selected regions. Anime clips are structurally poorly suited: "original content" and copyright rules exclude content copied from others, and copyrighted audio over 1 minute risks muting. Indonesia's CRP availability is not confirmed by an official source I could fetch.

### Cited Findings
- CRP eligibility (TikTok Support): account in good standing; authentic account info; 18+; at least 10,000 followers; at least 100,000 video views in the last 30 days; videos at least one minute long; personal account (not Business); account registered in an eligible region. Applications answered in about 3 days. — [TikTok Support: Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program)
- Rewards are based on qualified views and RPM; original content = designed, filmed and produced by you; ineligible examples include content copied completely from others or carrying others' watermarks; "Videos under the program that contain copyrighted music for over one minute are at risk of being muted." — [TikTok Support: Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program)
- Creator Academy: videos must follow Terms of Service, Community Guidelines, Creator Code of Conduct and Copyright Policy; cannot be Duets or Stitches; ads/sponsored content don't qualify; Spring 2025 "Additional Reward" added on top of Standard Reward; copyright pre-check of soundtracks recommended; Business accounts ineligible; appeals within 30 days; rewards may be taxable and TikTok issues no invoices. — [TikTok Creator Academy: Creator Rewards Program](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/creator-rewards-program)
- Other TikTok routes named in Creator Academy navigation/third-party summary: Creator Marketplace/brand deals, LIVE (gifts), TikTok Shop, Series (paywalled), Pulse ad-share. Eligibility numbers for each were not verified. — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/creator-rewards-program); [The Leap](https://www.theleap.co/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/)
- Indonesia: only unofficial aggregator/snippet sources list Indonesia among CRP-eligible countries (Facebook answers page, TikTok discover page); not verified against TikTok's official country list. — [TikTok discover snippet](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/what-countries-are-the-creativity-rewards-program-out-in); [Facebook snippet](https://www.facebook.com/fb-answers/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-available-countries-indonesia-official/?locale=zh_CN)
- Secondary sources differ on the follower floor in older years (200k views earlier vs 100k now); official page currently says 10k followers and 100k views. — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program); [TikTok creator video snippet](https://www.tiktok.com/@jackemathews/video/7348495721160559915?lang=en)
- Takedown compliance on TikTok for anime copyright requests reported at about 99.87% (CBR snippet, unverified). — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/anime-streaming-delete-anti-piracy-compliance/)

### Inferences
- Anime clip accounts are unlikely to qualify for CRP rewards while using others' footage; original commentary/analysis videos 1+ minute with licensed or royalty-free audio are the fit.
- Brand deals/TikTok Shop affiliate/LIVE gifts are separate from CRP and have their own policies, but still sit under the Copyright Policy; anime footage in sponsored or sales content adds risk (e.g. Kadokawa bars third-party product promotion in its guideline).

### Gaps
- No official confirmation of CRP availability in Indonesia as of 2026; no verified TikTok Shop affiliate or LIVE gift thresholds for Indonesia; TikTok's Copyright Policy and music-library (Commercial Music Library) page not fetched.

## 5. Indonesian law (UU Hak Cipta No. 28/2014; UU ITE)

### Takeaway
UU 28/2014 has no US-style open-ended fair use; it has enumerated limitations (Art. 43-46), with Art. 44 allowing use for education, research, scientific writing, report preparation, and critique/review ("kritik atau tinjauan") if the source is fully credited and the author's reasonable interests are not harmed. Commercial infringement carries up to 4 years/IDR 1bn (10 years/IDR 4bn for piracy), and offences are "delik aduan" (complaint-based). A November 2026 bill would revise the law for AI and news.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 44(1): use, taking, copying and/or modification of a work, fully or in substantial part, is not infringement if the source is mentioned in full, for: (a) education, research, scientific writing, report preparation, writing of criticism or review of an issue, as long as the reasonable interests of the author/holder are not harmed; (b) security/government/legislative/judicial; (c) lectures solely for education/science; (d) unpaid performances not harming the author's reasonable interests. — [UU 28/2014 text (Wikisource)](https://id.wikisource.org/wiki/Undang-Undang_Republik_Indonesia_Nomor_28_Tahun_2014); [BPK PDF](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/28018/UU%20Nomor%2028%20Tahun%202014.pdf)
- Art. 43(d): not infringement is "making and disseminating copyright content via ICT media that is non-commercial and/or benefits the author or related parties, or the author states no objection". The "non-commercial" qualifier is key for monetized channels. — [UU 28/2014 text (Wikisource)](https://id.wikisource.org/wiki/Undang-Undang_Republik_Indonesia_Nomor_28_Tahun_2014)
- Art. 46: private copying limited to one copy and does not cover substantial parts of books/music notation, databases, etc. — [UU 28/2014 text (Wikisource)](https://id.wikisource.org/wiki/Undang-Undang_Republik_Indonesia_Nomor_28_Tahun_2014)
- Art. 113: unauthorized economic-rights infringement for commercial use: para (1) up to 1 year and/or IDR 100m; para (2) up to 3 years and/or IDR 500m (rights incl. 9(1)c,d,f,h); para (3) up to 4 years and/or IDR 1bn (rights incl. 9(1)a,b,e,g); para (4) piracy up to 10 years and/or IDR 4bn. — [BPK PDF](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/28018/UU%20Nomor%2028%20Tahun%202014.pdf)
- Art. 120: offences under the law are "delik aduan" (prosecuted on complaint). — [UU 28/2014 text (Wikisource)](https://id.wikisource.org/wiki/Undang-Undang_Republik_Indonesia_Nomor_28_Tahun_2014)
- Art. 54-56: government may supervise/coordinate against infringing online content; anyone aware of online infringement for commercial use may report to the Minister, who verifies and can recommend the telecom/informatics ministry to block content or the whole service (court order required within 14 days if a whole site is blocked). — [UU 28/2014 text (Wikisource)](https://id.wikisource.org/wiki/Undang-Undang_Republik_Indonesia_Nomor_28_Tahun_2014)
- Protection term (life + 70 years for authors, per DJKI/regional summary). — [JDIH Semarang](https://jdih.semarangkota.go.id/artikel/view/undang-undang-nomor-28-tahun-2014-tentang-hak-cipta-perlindungan-dan-pengaturan-karya-kreatif-di-indonesia)
- Reuters (Nov 11 2026): Indonesia is considering copyright law revisions to make platforms pay publishers for news and set AI rules; Google, Meta and TikTok pushed back; TikTok urged preserving safe harbour and exempting assistive AI use from disclosure. Bill not passed as of that report. — [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/meta-tiktok-join-google-push-back-indonesias-planned-ai-copyright-law-revisions-2026-11-11/)

### Inferences
- A review/critique may fit Art. 44(1)(a) only if the source is fully credited, the use is proportionate, and it does not harm the owner's reasonable interests; re-uploading entire scenes or full recaps substituting for the original is unlikely to qualify, and monetization weakens any Art. 43(d) "non-commercial" argument. Indonesian courts' treatment of anime clips on YouTube is untested in what I found.
- Practically, Japanese rights holders enforce via platform notices; Indonesian criminal exposure is theoretically real for commercial infringement but requires a complaint (delik aduan), and in my research I found no reported Indonesian prosecution of an anime YouTuber.
- Rights are territorial: a Japanese licensor can pursue via Japanese law/platform processes regardless of Indonesian exceptions.

### Gaps
- UU ITE (UU 11/2008 as amended by UU 19/2016 and UU 1/2024): not researched; I have no verified citation for its relevance (e.g. Art. 32 on altering/transmitting electronic information, Art. 25 on intellectual property in electronic information) and did not confirm either article text. Do not rely on this.
- No Indonesian case law or DJKI guidance specifically on YouTube/TikTok clip channels, reaction, recap or AMV found.
- Art. 9 economic-rights letter mapping (which letters correspond to reproduction, adaptation, communication, etc.) not extracted; needed to say which penalty bracket (Art. 113(2) vs (3)) applies to uploads.
